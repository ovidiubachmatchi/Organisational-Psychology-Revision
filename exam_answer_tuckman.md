Question - Tuckman Study Design

(a) Design a study using observation to identify whether a group is forming or norming

I would use a structured non-participant observation for this and it would be overt. Structured because we already know from Tuckman what behaviours to expect at each stage so it makes sense to have the categories ready before starting rather than writing down whatever we notice. Non-participant because if the observer joins in with the group it could change how they behave and mess up the results. Overt because getting consent is important ethically even though it does mean people might act differently knowing they are being watched which is the Hawthorne effect.

The sample would be a project team in a workplace around 8 people selected through opportunity sampling since its hard to randomly assign which teams you observe in a real organisation. The team should either be newly formed or have been together for a few weeks because those are the time frames where you would expect forming or norming to show up. The observer would attend one of the teams regular meetings probably around an hour and sit away from the group so they do not interfere. They would use a behavioural checklist with event sampling to tally how often specific behaviours come up during the meeting.

The checklist needs two sets of behavioural categories. For forming the things to look for would be people being overly polite or cautious with each other and members looking for someone to take charge or asking questions about what they are supposed to be doing and generally just testing out what behaviours are acceptable. People in this stage worry about fitting in and there is a lot of uncertainty. For norming its quite different,you would expect to see conflict resolved and the group working together more smoothly and roles have been allocated and there is a clear leader and people follow agreed group norms. the discussions should feel more open and less awkward compared to forming.

To make sure the data is reliable I would have two observers at the meeting both using the same checklist independently. After the observation you compare what they both recorded to check inter-rater reliability. If both observers are getting similar results then you can be more confident the checklist is actually measuring what its supposed to.


(b) Explain the psychological evidence on which your study is based

The study is based on Tuckman model of group development. Tuckman reviewed 55 articles on small group development and identified four stages that groups go through in order which are forming storming norming and performing. Forming is where the group is new and members are uncertain about their role and the groups purpose,they test out behaviours and look for someone to lead them. Norming comes after the storming stage where conflict has happened and been dealt with,at this point the group has agreed on how to work together and roles are settled and there is a sense of unity.

Tuckman and Jensen later added a fifth stage called adjourning for when the group finishes its task and breaks apart.
