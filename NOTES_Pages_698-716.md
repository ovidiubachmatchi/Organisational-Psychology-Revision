# SECTION 1: Group Performance Across Cultures

> **TLDR**
> - Social loafing = doing less work in a group
> - **Earley (1989, 1993)**: collectivists DON'T loaf when in an **ingroup** but DO loaf with an **outgroup**
> - Individualists perform BETTER alone; collectivists perform BETTER with their ingroup
> - It's not just culture — it's about whether you **identify** with the group
> - Self-efficacy and anticipated rewards explain the difference

---

### Key Definitions

- **Social loafing** — reduced individual effort when placed in a group
- **Individualist culture** — values personal goals over group goals (e.g. USA)
- **Collectivist culture** — values group goals over personal goals (e.g. China)
- **Ingroup** — a group you identify with / feel part of
- **Outgroup** — a group you do NOT identify with
- **Efficacy** — belief in your ability to succeed
- **Cognitive bias** — assuming ingroup = similar to you, outgroup = different. Collectivists assume everyone in the ingroup will work better together; individualists assume everyone works better alone (because THEY prefer it)
- **Collective orientation** — placing group goals above self-interest
- **Self-interest motive** — believing group rewards should be based on individual contribution

---

### Earley (1989) — Culture and Social Loafing

- **WHO**: Earley
- **WHAT**: Studied US (individualist) and Chinese (collectivist) managers on a shared responsibility task
- **KEY FINDINGS**:
  - Social loafing WAS present in US managers but NOT in Chinese managers
  - Individualists performed worse in groups than alone
  - Collectivists performed better in groups than alone
  - Accountability did NOT affect loafing in collectivists — performance was HIGHEST in the highest shared responsibility groups
- **WHY it matters**: Shows social loafing is NOT universal — culture matters

#### Why the difference?

| Individualists | Collectivists |
|---|---|
| **Self-interest motive** | **Collective orientation** |
| Want rewards based on personal contribution | Place group goals above self-interest |
| If no one notices their work, they loaf | Take pride in group achievement |
| Group rewards feel unfair if not based on effort | High effort even without personal recognition |

---

### Earley's Process Model of Group Performance

- **Collectivists** feel more efficacy performing WITH a group (especially ingroup); **individualists** feel more efficacy performing ALONE
- Collectivists anticipate greater rewards + higher efficacy with an ingroup = perform better
- Individualists anticipate more rewards + higher efficacy alone = perform better alone than in ANY group
- The **cognitive bias** drives this: each assumes others share their preferences

---

### Earley (1993) — Testing the Process Model (Key Study)

- **WHO**: Earley
- **WHAT**: Extended the 1989 study — tested whether it's REALLY about culture or about ingroup vs outgroup

#### AIM

- Do collectivists perform worse with an **outgroup** than an **ingroup**?
- Do self-efficacy and anticipated rewards explain the effect?

#### METHOD

- **Sample**: 165 managers — 45 Israeli, 60 Chinese, 60 American (aged 25-40)
- **Design**: Lab experiment, 3 conditions:
  - Working **alone**
  - Working in a 10-person **outgroup** (differences emphasised)
  - Working in a 10-person **ingroup** (similarities emphasised)
- Participants were NOT in physical presence of their group — just TOLD they were part of one (they were in a room with other participants but told those were NOT their group members)
- **Task**: Simulated in-basket of 40 work items (memos, prioritising interviews, rating applications) in 60 minutes. Chosen because familiar to managers in their own workplaces.
- 5-minute practice trial first — output used to give participants an estimate of their expected performance
- **Measured**: Performance (correct items), individualism-collectivism scale (5-point), self-efficacy (0-100)

#### RESULTS

| | Alone | Outgroup | Ingroup |
|---|---|---|---|
| **China** (collectivist) | 18.49 | 18.37 | **23.83** |
| **USA** (individualist) | **24.55** | 16.21 | 16.57 |
| **Israel** (collectivist) | 20.79 | 18.63 | **23.18** |

- **Individualists** = best when ALONE
- **Collectivists** = best with INGROUP, worst with outgroup/alone
- Self-efficacy and anticipated performance **partially mediated** the effects of group condition and individualism-collectivism on performance
- Both group and individual self-efficacy were higher for collectivists (China, Israel) in ingroups than outgroups or alone

#### CONCLUSIONS

- Collectivists' efficacy increases with high group identification
- Collectivists CAN loaf — when working alone or with an outgroup
- **Type of group** (ingroup vs outgroup) may matter MORE than culture itself

📝 **Exam Bait**: Examiners love asking you to use the results table to explain findings. Practise reading the numbers.

---

### Evaluating Earley (1993)

**Strengths**:
- Used **quantitative data** (questionnaires, rating scales) — objective, replicable
- Extended 1989 study — showed "collectivists never loaf" was too simplistic
- More **holistic** — looked at interaction between culture, group type AND self-efficacy

**Weaknesses**:
- **Low ecological validity** — lab experiment, not a real workplace; strangers, not real ingroups
- **Nomothetic approach** — group averages hide individual reasons; an idiographic approach (case studies, interviews) would show WHY individuals behaved that way
- Earley himself questioned whether the artificial manipulation reflected real workplace dynamics

⚡ **Must-remember**: The key takeaway is that it's NOT just individualism vs collectivism — it's about **identification with the group**.

# SECTION 2: Electronic Performance Monitoring (EPM)

> **TLDR**
> - **Claypoole & Szalma (2019)**: Electronic monitoring IMPROVES performance on sustained attention tasks
> - Monitored participants detected more signals, made fewer errors, responded faster
> - Links to **social facilitation** — electronic "observer" works like a real one
> - BUT: constant monitoring could also cause stress and harm workers

---

### Key Definitions

- **Electronic Performance Monitoring (EPM)** — using technology (webcams, software) to track worker productivity
- **Vigilance** — sustained attention over an extended period
- **Evaluation apprehension** — anxiety caused by knowing you are being watched/judged

### Background Context

- Over 70% of US organisations use electronic systems to manage performance (APA, 2007)
- Research is mixed: EPM may improve OR impair performance depending on task difficulty — **Davidson & Henderson (2000)**
- **Aiello & Svec (1993)** found performance on a COMPLEX task was impaired by both human and electronic monitors vs working alone
- Gap in research: most EPM studies used simple tasks, NOT sustained attention tasks (driving, medical screening)

---

### Claypoole & Szalma (2019) — Key Study

- **WHO**: Claypoole & Szalma
- **WHAT**: Tested whether EPM produces social facilitation effects on a sustained attention task

#### AIM

- Does electronic presence improve performance on a vigilance task?

#### METHOD

- **Sample**: 106 undergraduates (65F, 41M, mean age 20.57, range 18-37) from a university in the south of the USA
- **Design**: Lab experiment, 2 conditions:
  - **Control** — no monitoring, no webcam, alone in room
  - **Electronic presence** — webcam on top of screen ("sends live feed to office next door") + video recorder behind screen ("records for later evaluation"). Nothing was actually recorded.
- Phones and watches surrendered at start
- 3-minute practice session first, then 24-minute vigilance task
- **Task**: Watch 2-digit numbers on screen (shown for 1000ms each). Press spacebar when numbers differ by 0 or +/-1 (e.g. 77, 65, 01). 5 critical signals per 6-minute period = 20 total. Task lasted no more than 1 hour.

#### RESULTS

| Measure | EPM Group | Control Group |
|---|---|---|
| Correct detections | **0.69** | 0.60 |
| False alarms | **0.006** (fewer = better) | 0.020 |
| Response time (ms) | **767** (faster) | 802 |

- EPM group was **better on ALL three measures**
- Performance declined over time in both groups (consistent with previous research)

#### CONCLUSIONS

- EPM can **improve** performance on boring but demanding sustained attention tasks
- Video-based monitoring is effective and less intrusive than other EPM forms

📝 **Exam Bait**: Examiners may ask you to link this to **evaluation apprehension** from social facilitation — the electronic monitor acts as an evaluator, improving simple/well-rehearsed task performance.

---

### Evaluating Claypoole & Szalma (2019)

**Strengths**:
- **Lab experiment** — standardised procedure, high control, only the monitor was manipulated = replicable
- **Quantitative data** — objective measures (response times, correct detections, false alarms), no experimenter bias

**Weaknesses**:
- **Unrepresentative sample** — undergrad students, not real workers; response bias from experiment website
- **Low task validity** — pressing spacebar for digit differences is NOT a real workplace task (driving, medical screening would be different)
- **Cultural differences** not considered — different countries have different levels of surveillance, different reactions to monitoring

**Ethics**:
- Confidentiality protected, informed consent gained, APA ethics approval
- **Deception** used — told recording was happening when it wasn't (but debriefed after)
- Credits for participation may have pressured students to stay
- Findings could lead to **constant workplace surveillance** = stress and harm

🔑 **Key Link**: **Bateson et al. (2006)** found even an image of EYES increased prosocial behaviour — people just need a CUE that they're being watched.

# SECTION 3: Conflict at Work

> **TLDR**
> - 4 levels: intra-individual, inter-individual, intra-group, inter-group
> - Causes = personality differences, competition for resources, poor communication, status differences
> - **Thomas-Kilmann**: 5 conflict-handling modes (competing, accommodating, avoiding, collaborating, compromising)
> - **Einarsen (1999)**: Bullying has 5 types, 4 phases, and 3 areas of causes
> - Conflict has BOTH individual and situational causes

---

### Why Conflict Happens

- Miscommunication between employees about goals and beliefs
- Belief that others are about to affect you negatively
- Three broad categories: **personal** (relationship breakdown), **task-related**, or **procedural** issues

---

### 4 Levels of Workplace Conflict

#### 1. Intra-individual conflict (WITHIN one person)

- Your goals/values clash with the organisation's
- Uncertainty about what's expected of you
- Feeling you're not good enough for the role
- Three types of goal conflict:
  - **Positive-positive**: choosing between two attractive options (two good job offers)
  - **Negative-negative**: choosing between two bad options (punishment OR hated task)
  - **Positive-negative**: one option has BOTH good and bad outcomes (great job, terrible location)

#### 2. Inter-individual conflict (BETWEEN two people)

- Disagreements between co-workers / managers
- Can be productive if handled well
- **Barki & Hartwick (2004)** — 3 components:
  - **Behavioural** (interference) — deliberately blocking someone's goals
  - **Cognitive** (disagreement) — different values/objectives
  - **Affective** (negative emotions) — anger, stress, frustration

#### 3. Intra-group conflict (WITHIN a single group)

- Between 2+ members of the SAME group
- Three types: **process** conflict (how to do it), **relationship** conflict (personality clashes), **task** conflict (what the task actually is)
- More likely with diverse backgrounds, ages, experience

#### 4. Inter-group conflict (BETWEEN different groups)

- Conflict between different teams/departments
- Can INCREASE group loyalty (positive) but also creates "us vs them" (negative)
- Linked to **social identity theory** — we exaggerate outgroup negatives, enhance ingroup positives
- Main cause: **competition for scarce resources** — linked to competitiveness, where the need for respect from others drives competition and conflict

⚡ **Memory trick for the 4 levels**: Think **"I-I-I-I"** = Intra-Individual, Inter-Individual, Intra-Group, Inter-Group. Goes from SMALLEST unit (inside one person) to LARGEST (between groups).

---

### Causes of Organisational Group Conflict — Miles (1980)

1. **Interdependence** — the more you HAVE to work together, the more conflict is likely
2. **Status inconsistencies** — unfair differences in time off, pay between groups
3. **Communication problems** — misunderstanding or misinterpreting each other
4. **Individual differences** — personality, skills, aggression, tolerance levels

**Other causes**:
- Lack of trust
- Scarce resources / budget competition
- Organisational changes (restructuring, redundancies, new team leaders)

### Causes of Interpersonal Conflict

- Competition for scarce resources (situational)
- Personality differences (individual)
- Clashes of values and interests
- Power and status differences (situational)
- Poor communication (most common cause)

📝 **Exam Bait**: The **individual vs situational debate** — conflict has BOTH types of causes. Changing the situation alone isn't enough; you also need to consider personality.

---

### Evaluating Conflict Explanations

- **Research support**: **Sherif (1962)** — boys at camp competing for prizes showed increased hostility, had to terminate study. Supports realistic conflict theory.
- **Application to real life**: Knowing causes helps organisations fix them (e.g. improve communication channels)
- **Cultural weakness**: Explanations are individualistic — status and power may not matter in collectivist cultures, reducing generalisability

---

### Thomas-Kilmann's 5 Conflict-Handling Modes

Two dimensions: **Assertiveness** (satisfying YOUR concerns) x **Cooperativeness** (satisfying THEIR concerns)

| Mode | Assertive? | Cooperative? | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Competing** | High | Low | Win at other's expense; defend your position |
| **Accommodating** | Low | High | Give in; neglect own concerns for others |
| **Avoiding** | Low | Low | Dodge conflict entirely; don't deal with it |
| **Collaborating** | High | High | Work together to find a win-win solution |
| **Compromising** | Medium | Medium | Middle ground; both give something up |

- Measured using the **Thomas-Kilmann Instrument (TKI)** — forced-choice questionnaire

⚡ **Memory trick**: **"C-A-A-C-C"** or think of a **2x2 grid** with assertiveness on one axis, cooperativeness on the other. Compromising sits in the MIDDLE.

---

### Evaluating Thomas-Kilmann

**Weakness**:
- TKI uses **forced-choice** answers — subject to **social desirability bias**
- Person may not agree with either option but must pick one — lowers validity

**Strength — Application**:
- Helps people understand their default style AND consider adapting to different situations
- BUT: assumes style can be LEARNED — it may be a fixed personality feature that's much harder to change than just learning about it

**Point**: Each person can use all 5 modes, but many are better at some than others due to personality or situational pressures

**Weakness — Nomothetic**:
- Assumes ALL conflict behaviour fits on 2 dimensions and 5 categories
- An idiographic approach (case studies, interviews) might reveal more nuanced responses

---

### Einarsen (1999) — Bullying at Work (Key Study)

- **WHO**: Einarsen
- **WHAT**: Review article summarising research on the nature, phases and causes of workplace bullying

#### 5 Types of Bullying

1. **Work-related** — changing/making tasks harder
2. **Social isolation**
3. **Personal attacks / ridicule** — insults, gossip, attacking private life
4. **Verbal threats** — criticised, yelled at, humiliated publicly
5. **Physical violence** or threats (rare — only ~2.4% of cases in Norwegian studies)

🔑 **Key point**: Most bullying is VERBAL, not physical.

#### 4 Phases of Bullying

1. **Aggressive behaviour** — subtle, indirect aggression toward someone
2. **Bullying** — aggression becomes more open, direct, frequent; victim less able to defend themselves
3. **Stigmatisation** — social stigma placed on victim; focus on their perceived flaws (low confidence, anxiety)
4. **Trauma** — severe impact on victim's physical and mental health

⚡ **Memory trick**: **"A-B-S-T"** = Aggression, Bullying, Stigmatisation, Trauma. Think: "ABST-ract cruelty that gets worse."

#### 3 Areas of Causes

- **Seign (1996)**: Interview study of 30 Irish victims — ALL blamed the personality of the bully

| The Bully | The Victim | The Workplace |
|---|---|---|
| Competition for status | Overachievers with unrealistic self-beliefs | Deficiencies in work design |
| Feelings of envy | Highly rigid | Deficiencies in leadership |
| Uncertainty about themselves | See themselves as more honest/punctual (annoying to others) | Socially exposed position of victim |
| (Bjorkqvist et al., 1994) | | Low moral standards in department |

#### CONCLUSION

- More empirical research needed — must focus on feelings of the person being victimised
- Need to distinguish between different types of bullying: **predatory** vs **dispute-related** bullying

---

### Evaluating Einarsen (1999)

**Weaknesses**:
- **Review article** — uses secondary sources, so you don't know if original research was robust = lower reliability
- **Victim-blaming** — suggesting victim personality is partly at fault is ethically problematic and could cause further psychological harm

**Strengths**:
- **Holistic** — considers individual factors (personality of bully AND victim) AND situational factors (workplace leadership, design, morality)
- **Application to real life** — identifying the 4 phases means early signs can be spotted sooner, reducing damage

📝 **Exam Bait**: Examiners LOVE the **reductionism vs holism** debate here. Einarsen is HOLISTIC because he considers multiple factors (individual + situational).

# Quick-Fire Q&A — Likely Exam Questions

### Q: Outline one difference between an individualist and collectivist society. [2]

- In an **individualist** society, people prioritise personal goals over group goals. In a **collectivist** society, people prioritise group goals and cooperation over self-interest.

---

### Q: Using Earley (1993), explain how culture affects group performance. [4]

- **Earley (1993)** found that **collectivists** (China, Israel) performed significantly better in an **ingroup** (e.g. Chinese mean = 23.83) than alone (18.49) or in an outgroup (18.37).
- **Individualists** (USA) performed best **alone** (24.55) and worst in groups (outgroup 16.21, ingroup 16.57).
- This is because collectivists have a **collective orientation** where group goals are valued, while individualists have a **self-interest motive** and prefer rewards based on personal contribution.

---

### Q: Evaluate Earley's (1993) study. [6-10]

- **Strength**: Quantitative data from structured questionnaires = objective, replicable
- **Strength**: Showed the relationship is complex (culture x group type x efficacy), not just collectivists never loaf
- **Weakness**: Lab experiment with strangers, not real workplaces — low ecological validity
- **Weakness**: Nomothetic approach — group averages hide individual differences; idiographic methods like case studies would show WHY people behaved as they did
- **Debate**: Cultural differences — most social loafing research uses individualist samples; Earley included collectivist cultures, making findings more generalisable

---

### Q: Describe the study by Claypoole & Szalma (2019). [6]

- **Aim**: Test whether EPM improves performance on a sustained attention task
- **Method**: Lab experiment; 106 undergrads; control (alone) vs electronic presence (webcam + video recorder). 24-min vigilance task detecting critical 2-digit signals.
- **Results**: EPM group had more correct detections (0.69 vs 0.60), fewer false alarms (0.006 vs 0.020), and faster response times (767ms vs 802ms).
- **Conclusion**: EPM can improve sustained attention performance; video-based monitoring is effective and less intrusive.

---

### Q: Describe the four levels of workplace conflict. [4-6]

- **Intra-individual**: conflict within one person (e.g. your values clash with the organisation's goals)
- **Inter-individual**: conflict between two people (e.g. co-workers disagreeing over resources)
- **Intra-group**: conflict within the same group (e.g. disagreements on how to complete a task)
- **Inter-group**: conflict between different groups/teams (e.g. departments competing for budget), linked to social identity theory

---

### Q: Outline Thomas-Kilmann's five conflict-handling modes. [6]

- **Competing**: assertive + uncooperative — pursue own goals at other's expense
- **Accommodating**: unassertive + cooperative — give up own concerns to satisfy others
- **Avoiding**: unassertive + uncooperative — dodge the conflict entirely
- **Collaborating**: assertive + cooperative — work together for a win-win solution
- **Compromising**: moderate on both — find a middle-ground solution

---

### Q: Describe Einarsen's (1999) findings on bullying. [6]

- **Types**: mostly verbal (social isolation, ridicule, insults); physical violence rare (~2.4%)
- **Phases**: starts with subtle aggression, escalates to open bullying, then stigmatisation of the victim, finally trauma affecting physical and mental health
- **Causes**: the bully (envy, status competition, insecurity), the victim (overachievers, rigid, seen as annoying), the workplace (poor leadership, work design flaws, low moral standards)

---

### Q: Evaluate Einarsen (1999) with reference to reductionism vs holism. [4]

- Einarsen's findings are **holistic** because they consider BOTH individual factors (personality of bully and victim) AND situational factors (workplace leadership, design, morality).
- This is a **strength** because it gives a more complete explanation and acknowledges that reducing bullying requires addressing workplace issues, not just blaming individuals.
- However, using a **review article** methodology means the original studies' robustness is unknown, lowering reliability.

---

### Q: Discuss the individual vs situational debate in relation to conflict at work. [4]

- **Individual factors**: personality differences (one person aggressive, another shy), the victim being an overachiever
- **Situational factors**: poor communication, organisational changes, competition for scarce resources, deficiencies in leadership
- This shows that just changing the situation (e.g. improving communication) may not be enough — you also need to consider workers' personalities
- A complete explanation of conflict needs BOTH perspectives
