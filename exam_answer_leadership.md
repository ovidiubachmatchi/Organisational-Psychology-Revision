a) Describe

Universalist theories are among the earliest explanations of leadership and they say that leaders have certain traits which make them effective no matter the situation. Great Person Theory argues that leaders are born with natural qualities and naturally rise during times of need. Charismatic Leadership looks at more specific traits like charisma, popularity and strong communication. Transformational Leadership is saying that leaders inspire followers to achieve more than they believed possible and Warrilow identified four characteristics: idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation and personal attention. All three theories argue that leadership depends on who the person is rather than the situation.

Heifetz and Laurie took a very different approach with adaptive leadership which focuses on what leaders do when an organisation faces challenges rather than who they are. Heifetz said the leader does not need to have all the answers. the point is to get the workforce to confront the problem themselves. He outlined six principles including getting on the balcony which means stepping back to see the bigger picture, identifying the adaptive challenge, regulating distress where leaders balance safety with pressure to drive change, maintaining disciplined attention, giving work back to employees and protecting voices from below which refers to whistleblowers and creative deviants. He used British Airways as an example since they had to confront their poor reputation and change from within.


(b) Evaluate

Universalist theories do have some evidence to support them. There are leaders who have come up during crises without any formal training which suggests there may be something innate involved. Cicero found a positive link between charismatic traits and workplace productivity through questionnaire data and Rubin found that transformational leaders were rated as highly effective by their teams. If these traits can be spotted then organisations can use psychometric testing during recruitment which many companies already do.

However these theories also have weaknesses. They are reductionist because leadership gets reduced to a small number of personal traits while ignoring things like workplace culture or the type of task or social class. Charismatic Leadership for example only considers internal characteristics and says nothing about how the wider environment might affect leadership. There are also ethical concerns as Stevens found that transformational leadership could lead to emotional stress,burnout and conflict which shows it should not always be seen as a positive approach.

Adaptive leadership does better in some of these areas. British Airways applied Heifetz principles during restructuring and saw improvements in both customer satisfaction and profits which shows the model can work in practice. It also takes situational factors into account which is something the universalist theories do not do. However Heifetz model is not without problems. Breaking leadership into six principles still does not account for individual workers personalities or the size of the organisation and the model is quite Western. Things like protecting whistleblowers and encouraging employees to challenge authority may not work in collectivist cultures where group harmony is valued more.

Both approaches also connect to the nature versus nurture debate, universalist theories and especially Great Person Theory fall on the nature side as Carlyle believed leaders were born with a gift that could not be developed through training. The problem with this is that it does not explain why some people with the right traits never become leaders or why others without them do through experience. Heifetz falls on the nurture side since all six of his principles are learned behaviours not things you are born with. In reality it is likely a combination of both,someone might naturally be more confident or assertive but without training and the right environment that alone would not make them a good leader.
